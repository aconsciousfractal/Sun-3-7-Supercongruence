# Claims, proof obligations and finite checks

The manuscript supplies the mathematical argument. This map identifies its
inputs and the precise role of the companion. TeX labels remain stable across
pagination; the [evidence guide](EVIDENCE.md) gives the computational scopes.

| Claim | Written argument | Computational support and its limit |
|---|---|---|
| Both sums satisfy Sun's Conjecture 3.7 modulo p² for every prime p≠2,5 | Theorem `thm:main`, with separate split and inert proofs and a direct calculation at p=3 | `prime_regression.csv`: 301 admissible primes through 1999 using recurrences. `original_sums.json`: 44 primes through 199 from the definitions. These are finite regressions. |
| The complete finite invariant vanishes modulo p² | Lemma `lem:invariant`: exact differential residual and the separately computed coefficients at p and 2p | `finite_invariants.json` records counts and resonance outcomes after the constructor checks complete polynomials in a declared finite prime window. `algebra.json` includes selected symbolic identities. |
| A zero at a nonsingular unit parameter lifts from p to p² | Lemma `lem:lifting`: the zero has exactly multiplicity two and its second derivative is a unit | The invariant panel tests instances; `finite_summary.json` records the count of parameter shifts checked by `finite_audit.py`. `original_sums.json` stores the unshifted values. The generic multiplicity and lifting proofs are in the text. “Nonsingular” here concerns the differential equation. |
| Eta data identify each period before CM specialization | `lem:eta`, `lem:period`, `prop:parameters` and Appendix `app:modular`: modularity, cusp holomorphy, sufficient coefficient bound, differential equation and formal uniqueness | `modular_certificates.json` records the finite coefficients and exact eta data. They are sufficient only together with the proved modular hypotheses and bound. |
| The period characters and divisors hold on the projective quotient | `lem:characters`, `prop:poles`: explicit Atkin–Lehner transformations and cusp/pole calculation | Exact eta data and selected symbolic rational identities test the formulas. Bounded transformations do not prove the general divisor argument. |
| The individual degree-p endpoint is paid independently | `lem:endpoint`: binomial divisibility for a; the known Q endpoint follows from Mao Theorem 1.4, with the alternative deduction from Straub Theorem 3.2 and A_F(n)=(−1)^n Q_n retained | Sequence and prime checks test coefficient congruences. They are not the proof of the endpoint for every prime. |
| The Hecke polynomial has integral coefficients and the required generic degree bound | `lem:hecke`: Beukers Lemma 3.7, cusp holomorphy, pole bound, cyclotomic integrality and triangular descent in the rational parameter | Six explicit polynomials at p=7,11,29 in `hecke_polynomials.json`. Examples do not prove integral descent or the degree bound for arbitrary p. |
| The finite polynomial identity may be specialized at the two rational parameters | `thm:specialization`: the two-term kernel modulo p², top coefficient modulo p and endpoint; the degree-p argument is attributed to the coefficient comparison in the proof of Beukers Theorem 6.1 and adapted locally | Coefficient checks for the reconstructed Hecke polynomials. Evaluation follows a finite identity and division by units; no infinite series is evaluated at a p-adic unit. |
| All split classes give the stated rational traces | `lem:cm`, `lem:forms`, `prop:split`: exact CM values, determinant-one and determinant-two matrices, character sign and prior choice of the unit embedding | `CM_matrices.json` and exact factors in `hecke_polynomials.json`. The generic Bézout constructions and binary-form classification are in the proof. |
| Finite transport, Dixon and Legendre identities connect both original sums to the Hasse polynomial modulo p | `lem:transport`, `lem:dixon`, `lem:hasse`, `lem:legendre-square`, `cor:quartic`: p-integral parameters, a unit transport factor, finite binomial identities and a polynomial identity over finite fields | `finite_Hasse_bridges.json`, `Hasse_polynomials.json` and `exact_Legendre_squares.json`. The statements over field extensions follow from polynomial identities, not informal substitution into a character sum. |
| The explicit degree-five map satisfies Φ²=[−5] and remains valid for every p>5 | Theorem `thm:isogeny` and Appendix `app:isogeny`: four fixed identities, both coordinates, denominators, discriminant and resultant; smooth-projective extension and preservation of O | `isogeny_certificate.json` stores fixed polynomials; `isogeny_summary.json` records residual counts and outcomes of their full expansion. Uniform reduction also needs the written denominator and nondegeneration argument. `finite_curves.json` records counts from separate sampled checks. |
| Every inert prime gives zero for each sum modulo p² | Proposition `prop:inert`: cyclic ordinary geometric p-torsion contradicts Φ²=[−5]; Deuring's criterion, finite polynomial bridges and local lifting then apply | Hasse, curve, invariant and original-sum panels test the chain. Integer trace over an extension is not asserted to vanish. |
| The quartic factor criterion and stability under t↦t+pr follow | `cor:factor`, `cor:stability`: simple Legendre zeros and Taylor expansion; the split converse uses the already proved main theorem | Hasse polynomials and local-lift tests. These corollaries remain downstream and supply no premise to the main proof. |

The [source lock](SOURCES.md) distinguishes theorem imports from local proofs
and related identities. The theorem excludes 2 and 5 exactly as Sun's source
does; p=3 is checked separately. Equality modulo p³ is not claimed and fails
at p=7. The companion is not a formal proof and does not certify novelty,
priority or independent human specialist acceptance.
